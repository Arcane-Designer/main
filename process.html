Process

How a redesign works, from hello to handoff.

About two to three weeks in all. Here’s every step, what I’ll need from you, and when you pay.

  1. Send me your website

    Day one

    Use the contact form or email me a link to your current site. I’ll look it over and reply with what I’d change and what it would cost, usually within two business days.

    If your site is in decent shape and a redesign wouldn’t be worth your money, I’ll say that.

  2. Deposit and questionnaire

    Day two or three

    Once you’re in, I send an invoice for half the price. Card or bank transfer, through Stripe.

    Then a short questionnaire: who your customers are, what you want them to do on your site, sites you like, colors you can’t stand, and anything that must stay. It takes about 20 minutes and it’s the most useful 20 minutes of the project.

  3. Three designs to click through

    About a week in

    I design three different directions for your site and send them as working web pages. Not flat pictures. You can open them on your phone, click between pages and get a real feel for each one.

    Pick the one you like best. Mix and match is fine too (“the colors from the second one, the layout from the first”).

  4. One round of changes

    A few days

    Tell me what to adjust, all in one list. I make the changes and send it back for a final look before anything is built.

    Need another round after that? It happens. Small tweaks are fine. A big change of direction gets a quick quote first, so there are no surprises.

  5. I build it on your platform

    About a week

    I rebuild your site on the platform you already use: Squarespace, Wix, WordPress, Shopify or similar. Your domain, hosting and accounts stay exactly where they are.

    You add me as a collaborator with my own login. You never share your password with me.

  6. Launch

    Two to three weeks from the start

    We pick a quiet time, I switch the new design on and check every page on a phone and a computer. The second half of the invoice is due at launch.

  7. A week of fixes, then I hand back the keys

    The week after launch

    For a week after launch, anything you notice that isn’t right, I fix. A typo, a photo that crops oddly, a button that should say something else.

    Then you remove my access with one click. I send you a short checklist so you can confirm nothing of mine is left on your account. That’s the end of the project, and the website is all yours.

  8. A review, if you’re happy

    Whenever you have a minute

    If you liked working with me, I’ll ask for a short Google review. It’s the main way a one-person business like this gets found.

Yes, I use AI. Here’s how.

AI tools are part of how I work. They help me research your industry, try more layout ideas than I could sketch by hand, build the clickable designs faster and catch mistakes before you do.

What they don’t do is make the decisions. I choose the direction, write and edit the words with you, and check every page myself before you see it. You get the speed of the tools and the judgment of a person who’s accountable for the result.

Good to know

Questions people ask.

Is there a monthly fee?

No. You pay for the project and that’s it. Your hosting and platform bills (Squarespace, Wix, WordPress hosting and so on) stay exactly as they are, paid by you to them.

Do you host my website?

No, and that’s on purpose. I build on the platform you already use, so you never have to move your site or depend on me to keep it running.

Which platforms do you work with?

Squarespace, Wix, WordPress, Shopify, Webflow, GoDaddy and Showit cover most of what I see. If you’re on something else, send me the link and I’ll tell you whether it works.

Do I have to give you my password?

No. Almost every platform lets you add me as a collaborator with my own login. When the project is done, you remove me with one click, and I send you a short checklist to confirm nothing of mine is left behind.

How long does it take?

Most projects take two to three weeks from questionnaire to launch. The biggest factor is how quickly feedback comes back.
